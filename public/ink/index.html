Ink ingredients

What's in tattoo ink?

Every tattoo ink is a mix of pigment particles suspended in a carrier, plus additives that keep it stable. Here is what each part does — and how to find out what's in a specific bottle.

The three main components

  • Pigments — the insoluble particles that stay in the skin and create color. They may be organic (carbon-based, such as many bright reds and yellows) or inorganic (mineral, such as titanium dioxide whites and iron oxides).
  • Carrier — the liquid that holds pigment evenly and carries it into the skin, commonly water with ingredients such as glycerin, alcohols or witch hazel.
  • Additives — binders, thickeners, surfactants and preservatives that control flow, shelf life and consistency.

How to read an ink label or safety data sheet

Pigments are usually identified by a Colour Index name and number (for example "Pigment Red 254, CI 56110") and a CAS number. Looking those up tells you the actual chemical — including whether it contains metals.

How tattoo ink is regulated in the US

Tattoo inks are regulated by the FDA as cosmetics. The Modernization of Cosmetics Regulation Act of 2022 (MoCRA) added new requirements for manufacturers, including facility registration and product listing.
